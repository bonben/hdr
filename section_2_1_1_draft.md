## Interest in Short Codes, Relevance of Polar Codes

In the context of 5G and future 6G networks, Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communications (URLLC) rely heavily on short blocklength codes. For these specific constraints, Polar codes have proven to be highly effective, often outperforming Low-Density Parity-Check (LDPC) codes. However, decoding them efficiently remains a challenge. The Successive Cancellation (SC) decoder is computationally efficient but suffers from poor error-correction performance on short blocks. Conversely, the Successive Cancellation List (SCL) decoder offers excellent performance but at the cost of high computational complexity and memory usage.

To address this trade-off, the Successive Cancellation Flip (SCF) decoding algorithm was proposed. It attempts an initial SC decoding and, if a failure is detected via Cyclic Redundancy Check (CRC), it flips the least reliable decisions and re-attempts decoding. While SCF achieves performance close to SCL with average complexity close to SC, it suffers from a major drawback: high worst-case latency. In standard SCF, each flip requires a complete re-decoding of the frame from the beginning, leading to unacceptable delays for latency-critical applications when multiple flips are needed.

I am currently planning a collaboration with ETS Montreal (Pascal Giard) to implement the \textit{Generalized Restart Mechanism} (GRM) for software polar decoders~\cite{Sagitov2025}. Unlike standard SCF, GRM memorizes the internal states (LLRs and partial sums) of the decoder during the initial attempt. When a flip is required, the decoder does not restart from scratch but resumes execution from the exact point of the flip, reusing the stored valid states. This technique significantly reduces the computational overhead of subsequent attempts. Preliminary studies suggest that GRM can reduce the average execution time by 25\% to 60\% compared to standard SCF, without degrading error-correction performance.

The primary challenge of this work lies in the efficient software implementation of GRM. Storing internal states introduces a trade-off: it reduces generating computations but increases memory bandwidth usage and footprint. Efficient memory management is therefore critical. Furthermore, the non-linear execution flow of a restart-based algorithm poses challenges for vectorization (SIMD) and instruction pipelining, which are standard optimization techniques in high-performance software decoders. A key objective of this collaboration is to integrate a robust, optimized C++ implementation of the GRM-SCF decoder into the open-source \textbf{AFF3CT} library. This will provide the research community with a high-performance reference implementation, facilitating further reproducible research.

These developments also have promising applications in the emerging field of quantum error correction. Quantum polar codes, which are gaining attention for their capacity-achieving properties, typically rely on a classical decoding stage similar to SCL~\cite{Gong2024}. A new construction of quantum polar codes based on weight polarization (PW) has been proposed, eliminating the need for entanglement assistance. Adapting the GRM-SCF approach to these quantum codes could significantly reduce the complexity of the classical decoding step. By replacing the computationally intensive SCL decoder with a lower-complexity SCF variant enhanced by GRM, we could achieve a more efficient implementation suitable for the stringent latency constraints of quantum error correction cycles. exploring this adaptation, potentially leveraging deep learning techniques for code construction as presented in my previous work~\cite{leonardon:hal-03291030}, represents a key perspective for my future research.
